Basaltic flows,
planetary surfaces.

Volcanology · Remote Sensing · Planetary Science

I study how lava moves and what it leaves behind. Using the 2021–2023 Fagradalsfjall eruptions, I investigate emplacement dynamics and surface morphology to understand how lava travels on Earth and shapes volcanic histories on Mars and Venus.

I'm a PhD candidate in Geosciences at the University of Arizona, with joint affiliation in Lunar and Planetary Sciences, advised by Dr. Christopher Hamilton. My research combines volcanology, remote sensing, and planetary science, using Earth's most active lava fields to build frameworks for reading volcanic histories across the solar system. As an NSF GRFP Fellow and member of the NASA VERITAS Discovery mission team, I work at the boundary between terrestrial hazard science and planetary exploration. Outside the lab and field, I'm a trail and marathon runner, rock climber, and drone pilot.

Research Focus

From emplacement
to surface record.

Dissertation

Fagradalsfjall Lava Flow Emplacement

Documenting how basaltic flows from Iceland's 2021–2023 eruptions were emplaced and how their surface morphology records thermal and rheological history, providing a framework for interpreting ancient lava fields elsewhere in the solar system.

  • Tuohy M. et al., "The Impact of Dynamic Topography on Lava Facies: A Time-Series Analysis of Fill-and-Spill Processes at Fagradalsfjall," GeoDaze Symposium, Tucson, AZ, Mar 2025
  • Tuohy M. et al., "A geomorphological analysis of the 2021–2023 Fagradalsfjall eruption series," IAVCEI Scientific Assembly, Geneva, Jul 2025

Planetary Analogs

Volcanic Surfaces on Mars & Venus

Translating terrestrial lava flow models to planetary contexts. Contributing to the NASA VERITAS Discovery mission, applying high-resolution surface mapping methods to Venusian volcanism.

  • Whitten J.L. et al., "Simulating volcanic flow mapping using the VERITAS Iceland 2023 field campaign synthetic aperture radar data," LPSC 56, The Woodlands, TX, Mar 2026

Modeling

Flow Dynamics & Surface Disruption

Developing Python-based models of lava flow surface disruption incorporating slope mechanics, eddy dissipation, and thermal effects to predict morphologic outcomes from rheological inputs.

UAV Systems & Near-Surface Geophysics

Remote Sensing, Geophysics & Instrumentation

Integrating drone-based SfM photogrammetry, DEM analysis, radar datasets, hyperspectral imaging, and UAV-GPR for lava flow characterization, subsurface investigation, and explosive ordnance detection.
